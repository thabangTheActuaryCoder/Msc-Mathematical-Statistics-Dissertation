\subsection{Monte Carlo Joint-Distribution Validation}
\label{annexure:mc_joint_validation}

The figures in this subsection provide detailed bivariate and
multivariate diagnostics comparing the joint distributional structure of
the training data with that of the Monte Carlo sample.  These
diagnostics support the validation conclusions summarised in
Section~\ref{sec:simulated_input_validation_ch4}.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{Thesis/Chapter 4/Figures/fig_mc_joint_continuous_pairs.png}
\caption{Continuous variable pairs: training (top row, blue) versus
Monte Carlo (bottom row, red).  All pairwise Pearson correlations are
negligible in both samples, confirming that the product-marginal
simulation does not discard meaningful continuous dependence.}
\label{fig:mc_joint_continuous_pairs_annex}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{Thesis/Chapter 4/Figures/fig_mc_joint_crosstabs.png}
\caption{Conditional distributions of selected categorical variable
pairs: training (solid bars) versus Monte Carlo (hatched bars).  The
conditional proportions are approximately uniform across the categories
of the conditioning variable in both regimes, indicating effective
independence among the training-data categoricals.}
\label{fig:mc_joint_crosstabs_annex}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{Thesis/Chapter 4/Figures/fig_threeway_crosstabs.png}
\caption{Three-way cross-tabulations of the four key categorical
variables: training (blue) versus Monte Carlo (red).  The maximum
absolute difference between the training and Monte Carlo joint
proportions is $0.012$, and the mean absolute differences are between
$0.003$ and $0.004$.}
\label{fig:threeway_crosstabs_annex}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{Thesis/Chapter 4/Figures/fig_energy_distance_test.png}
\caption{Energy distance permutation test: null distribution and
observed statistic.  The observed energy distance is $-0.022$ with a
permutation $p$-value of $0.730$, indicating that the joint
distributions are indistinguishable by this non-parametric criterion.}
\label{fig:energy_distance_annex}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.70\textwidth]{Thesis/Chapter 4/Figures/fig_tsne_joint_overlay.png}
\caption{t-SNE projection of training versus Monte Carlo joint
distributions.  The point clouds are thoroughly intermixed across all
identified clusters, confirming that the Monte Carlo profiles do not
occupy novel regions of the feature space.}
\label{fig:tsne_joint_overlay_annex}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{Thesis/Chapter 4/Figures/fig_mc_input_distributions.png}
\caption{Marginal distributions of all 11 raw predictor variables:
training versus Monte Carlo.  The simulated marginals closely match the
training-data marginals for all variables.}
\label{fig:mc_input_distributions_annex}
\end{figure}
